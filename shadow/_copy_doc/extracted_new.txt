Parasolv features
Every tool a shadow study needs, all available inside SketchUp.
Card 1 — Intuitive scenario based setup
Parasolv works by learning what shadow conditions you want to compare—from a city neighborhood before and after a new tower to a backyard before and after a second story addition. With simple, intuitive setup options you will be ready to go in minutes. 
Card 2 — Incredible Flexibility
Which days do you want to study?  Which daylight hours are important to you? Want to study the full year, or just a single season?  Parasolv can accommodate whatever parameters your study needs. 
Card 3 — Time Step Graphics
Generate an image series showing the movement of shadows over time, with color coding to clearly differentiate the new shadows from existing ones.
Card 4 — Shadow Aggregates
See the full range of locations where shadows fall across any day, season, or even a full year — all layered into one informative and visually stunning graphic, color coded to show the range of shadow durations.
Card 5 — Quantitative Analysis
Need to know detailed information about the effects of shadow on specific area such as a city park or a bedroom window?  Just assign it as a receptor and Parasolv can generate a full and customizable impact report.
Card 6 — PV Assessment (beta)
Evaluate the effect of shadow on photovoltaic installations using historical solar output data from select systems to gain a real-world understanding of the .
Card 7 — Shadow Shaper (beta)
Test, evaluate and refine massing volumes and see their shadow effects on any area that may be sensitive to shadow.  A powerful design and diagnostic tool.  
Card 8 — Advanced Workflow Tools
Advanced tools to expedite workflows for large projects: Batch Processing to sequence multiple outputs to run concurrently and Mask Mode which allows multiple processes to leverage the same shadow data for greater speed and efficiency.

Quickstart

Step 01
Install the plugin
Download Parasolv from your account page, double-click the .rbz, and restart SketchUp. The Parasolv panel appears in the Extensions menu.
Screenshot: SketchUp extensions menu with Parasolv highlighted
Step 02
Set your location & date
Open the Setup panel, confirm the model location, and choose the day (or range) you want to analyze. Parasolv uses SketchUp’s native geolocation so you don’t need to re-enter it.
Screenshot: Setup panel with location + date selector
Step 03
Classify your model
Tag buildings and obstructions as sources, and the surfaces you care about (neighboring facades, public space, gardens) as receptors. Think of it as telling the plugin “what’s casting shadow” vs. “what you’re measuring.”
Screenshot: Classifier panel with entity list + colored preview
Step 04
Run your first study
Hit Run. Shadow Fan overlays appear on the model within seconds for a single day; a full-year study takes a minute or two depending on receptor count.
Screenshot: Model with live Shadow Fan overlay
Step 05
Read the results
The Results panel lists each receptor with sun-hours and shadow-hours. The gradient overlay on the model shows where exposure is highest and lowest at a glance.
Screenshot: Results panel with receptor table + gradient legend
Step 06
Export for your report
Pro and Studio tiers export CSVs ready for planning submissions or client decks. Lite users can screenshot the viewport overlay.
Screenshot: CSV preview with per-receptor metrics
Studio track
Step 01
Grid resolution & sampling
Studio users should tune grid resolution before running compliance studies. As a rule of thumb: 0.25 m for facade receptors where local planning guidance requires sub-metre accuracy, 1.0 m for open-space studies. See the methodology reference for per-jurisdiction notes.
Screenshot: Analysis Settings with grid resolution + timestep controls
Step 02
Batch configuration
Queue multiple massing variants in one run. Each entry can override the source set, date range, or envelope constraints. Batches serialize to JSON — check them into your project repo for reproducibility.
Screenshot: Batch configuration JSON with queued scenarios
Step 03
Design Envelope workflow
Define the shadow constraint (e.g. “≥ 2 hrs sun on Mar 21, 10:00–16:00, on this facade”), then generate the maximum-buildable volume that satisfies it. The result is a SketchUp solid you can union with your site boundary for developable-area studies.
Screenshot: Envelope generation with constraint dialog
Step 04
Scenario comparison
Layer any number of scenarios over a baseline. The comparison view highlights deltas per receptor — useful for planning appeals where you need to demonstrate that a scheme does not degrade existing conditions.
Screenshot: Scenario diff view with +/- deltas per receptor
Step 05
PV assessment methodology
PV output estimates use clear-sky irradiance combined with the Parasolv solar model. Calibrate against TMY data for your region if you need compliance-grade numbers; otherwise the default profile is good for feasibility-stage estimates.
Screenshot: PV surface heatmap with irradiance legend
Step 06
CSV schema & automation
Exported CSVs follow a documented schema: receptor_id, date, sun_hours, shadow_hours, met_criteria. Hook them into Excel, Grasshopper, or Python for bespoke reporting pipelines.
Screenshot: CSV schema reference with column definitions
